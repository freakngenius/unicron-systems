The four zones, defined

Commodity
Sell licenses to humans who do the work. Buyers are price-shopping. Compression is the thesis. AI accelerates it.
Vanishing Tools
High autonomy, low ownership. Cursor, Replit Agent, Copilot. The product gets dramatically better; pricing power gets dramatically worse.
Labor Shops
Outcome-owning, but human-powered. BPOs, agencies, managed services. AI compresses costs but the operating shape stays human.
Agent-Native Frontier
Outcome-owning AND agent-powered. The agent IS the operator. Schmidt's "roofing co." Unicron's destination.

Three migration paths for our top candidates

Public Adjuster
O1 / A2  →  O5 / A4
From "claims software for adjusters" to an agent-run virtual adjusting firm that takes 30% of recovered claims. Massive upside. Hard licensing wall (state-by-state PA licensure). Worth 2 hours of regulatory research before committing.
Property Intelligence
O3 / A3  →  O5 / A4
From "property data feed" to an agent-broker firm that continuously reconnoiters parcels and brokers signals. Few licensing walls. Data + distribution loop is the moat. Cleanest fit for agent-native shape.
Mold Remediation
O1 / A2  →  O4 / A3 (capped)
Capped at managed-ops because the work itself is physical. You can compress everything around the work — quoting, scheduling, comms, billing — but the trucks and crews stay human. Best treated as a tools play, not an agent-native firm.

The one-line test

For each candidate vertical, complete this sentence in one line. If you can't, it's not demo-able by June 2.

01
"At submission, this demo shows an L___ agent…"
02
"…doing [specific workflow] end-to-end…"
03
"…with humans only doing [specific intervention]…"
04
"…and we capture [outcome / revenue share] per unit."

If steps 1–3 are clear but step 4 is "we sell software for $X/seat," you're in the vanishing-tools zone. Reposition or drop.

The companion ladder — where does ownership flow back to?

The autonomy ladder tells you how the work runs. The reciprocity ladder tells you whether what you build is a living thing or an extractive one. A forest is not only invocation — it is also what the forest gives back to the soil. A firm that only concentrates outcome ownership is the extraction engine with a forest metaphor painted on it.

Unlocks referral, data, and labeling loops without ads.
R4 · Agent budget & decay
Computational labor is priced like labor — agents accumulate budgets, rest, and expire. Spent cycles aren't stolen, they're compensated on the substrate.
The organism itself. Specialist agents earn / decay budgets the operator respects.
Agents can refuse work when under-budgeted. No perpetual motion.
R5 · Seven-generations flow
A permanent, non-rescindable slice of outcome goes to the commons — successor users, future cohorts, the vertical's public good. A tithe, not a grant.
The future. The community. Unborn users. A named public benefit.
Structural, in the cap table or a standing smart contract. Not a PR line item.
Pair this with the 5×5 above: a vertical placed at O5/A4 but anchored only at R1 is still extractive. The agent-native frontier only stays alive if something material flows back. Unicron's target is O5/A4 × R3–R4 at submission, with a credible 90-day path to R5.

The two questions that narrow the field.

How autonomous? Pick a rung you can credibly demo by June 2. Anything above L4 is currently marketing, not engineering. Anything below L3 is a tool, not an agent.

Who owns the outcome? Selling seats puts you in the vanishing-tools zone where Amjad is right and the floor falls out. Owning outcomes — even partially — moves you into the frontier where Schmidt's economics live.

And where does it flow back to? A frontier-zone company with R1 reciprocity is still an extraction engine. The living firm is the one where part of the outcome, by design, does not stay at the top.

Together they form a single decision: pick a quadrant, pick a reciprocity rung, then pick a vertical. Most teams pick a vertical first and end up in commodity by accident. We can do this in the other order.

The contest reward isn't $1M for the prettiest demo. It's $1M for the company shape that didn't exist 18 months ago — and whose shape is worth copying.
